# Master Timeline — Cyber Resilience Act

A **product-cybersecurity** law for products with digital elements (hardware + software that
connect directly or indirectly to a device or network) placed on the EU market. Directly
applicable across all Member States (a Regulation, not a Directive). It rolls out in phases —
the trap is treating it as one 2027 deadline.

| Date | Applies to | Detail |
|------|-----------|--------|
| 10 Dec 2024 | — | Regulation entered into force |
| 11 Jun 2026 | Member States | Rules on **notification of conformity assessment bodies** apply |
| **11 Sep 2026** | Manufacturers | **Article 14 reporting** — report actively exploited vulnerabilities and severe incidents via the ENISA **Single Reporting Platform**. **24 h** early warning → **72 h** full notification → **final report** within **14 days** (vulnerabilities) or **1 month** (severe incidents) |
| **11 Dec 2027** | All in-scope actors | **Full application** — essential cybersecurity requirements (Annex I), conformity assessment, CE marking, technical documentation, vulnerability handling across the support period |

## Penalty tiers

| Breach | Ceiling |
|--------|---------|
| Essential requirements (Annex I) / core manufacturer obligations | **€15 M or 2.5%** of worldwide annual turnover |
| Other obligations (authorised rep, technical docs, CE marking) | **€10 M or 2%** |
| Incorrect/incomplete/misleading information to authorities | **€5 M or 1%** |

## State of play (mid-2026 — check before relying on the conformity route)
- **No CRA-designated notified bodies** yet listed in NANDO.
- **No CRA harmonised standard** yet in the Official Journal; CEN/CENELEC still drafting.
- ENISA Single Reporting Platform in functional/security testing toward 11 Sep 2026.

**Implication:** the internal-documentation and reporting-readiness work can (and must) start
now; the third-party conformity route for higher-class products may be temporarily blocked.
